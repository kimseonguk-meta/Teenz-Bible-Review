### TITLE EN: Paul’s New Crew and a Surprise Pep Talk
### TITLE KO: 바울의 새 크루와 깜짝 격려

[MSG 0] range=1-4 header='Corinth'
After Athens, Paul went to Corinth. That is where he discovered Aquila, a Jew born in Pontus, and his wife, Priscilla. They had just arrived from Italy, part of the general expulsion of Jews from Rome ordered by Claudius. Paul moved in with them, and they worked together at their common trade of tentmaking. But every Sabbath he was at the meeting place, doing his best to convince both Jews and Greeks about Jesus.

[MSG 1] range=5-6 header=None
When Silas and Timothy arrived from Macedonia, Paul was able to give all his time to preaching and teaching, doing everything he could to persuade the Jews that Jesus was in fact God's Messiah. But no such luck. All they did was argue contentiously and contradict him at every turn. Totally exasperated, Paul had finally had it with them and gave it up as a bad job. "Have it your way, then," he said. "You've made your bed; now lie in it. From now on I'm spending my time with the other nations."

[MSG 2] range=7-8 header=None
He walked out and went to the home of Titius Justus, a God-fearing man who lived right next to the Jews' meeting place. But Paul's efforts with the Jews weren't a total loss, for Crispus, the meeting-place president, put his trust in the Master. His entire family believed with him.

[MSG 3] range=8-11 header=None
In the course of listening to Paul, a great many Corinthians believed and were baptized. One night the Master spoke to Paul in a dream: "Keep it up, and don't let anyone intimidate or silence you. No matter what happens, I'm with you and no one is going to be able to hurt you. You have no idea how many people I have on my side in this city." That was all he needed to stick it out. He stayed another year and a half, faithfully teaching the Word of God to the Corinthians.

[MSG 4] range=12-13 header=None
But when Gallio was governor of Achaia province, the Jews got up a campaign against Paul, hauled him into court, and filed charges: "This man is seducing 12-13 But when Gallio was governor of Achaia province, the Jews got up a campaign against Paul, hauled him into court, and filed charges: "This man is seducing people into acts of worship that are illegal."

[MSG 5] range=14-16 header=None
Just as Paul was about to defend himself, Gallio interrupted and said to the Jews, "If this was a matter of criminal conduct, I would gladly hear you out. But it sounds to me like one more Jewish squabble, another of your endless hairsplitting quarrels over religion. Take care of it on your own time. I can't be bothered with this nonsense," and he cleared them out of the courtroom.

[MSG 6] range=17 header=None
Now the street rabble turned on Sosthenes, the new meeting-place president, and beat him up in plain sight of the court. Gallio didn't raise a finger. He could not have cared less.

[MSG 7] range=18 header='Ephesus'
Paul stayed a while longer in Corinth, but then it was time to take leave of his friends. Saying his good-byes, he sailed for Syria, Priscilla and Aquila with him. Before boarding the ship in the harbor town of Cenchrea, he had his head shaved as part of a vow he had taken.

[MSG 8] range=19-21 header=None
They landed in Ephesus, where Priscilla and Aquila got off and stayed. Paul left the ship briefly to go to the meeting place and preach to the Jews. They wanted him to stay longer, but he said he couldn't. But after saying good-bye, he promised, "I'll be back, God willing."

[MSG 9] range=21-22 header=None
From Ephesus he sailed to Caesarea. He greeted the church there, and then went on to Antioch, completing the journey.

[MSG 10] range=23 header=None
After spending a considerable time with the Antioch Christians, Paul set off again for Galatia and Phrygia, retracing his old tracks, one town after another, putting fresh heart into the disciples.

[MSG 11] range=24-26 header=None
A man named Apollos came to Ephesus. He was a Jew, born in Alexandria, Egypt, and a terrific speaker, eloquent and powerful in his preaching of the Scriptures. He was well-educated in the way of the Master and fiery in his enthusiasm. Apollos was accurate in everything he taught about Jesus up to a point, but he only went as far as the baptism of John. He preached with power in the meeting place. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and told him the rest of the story.

[MSG 12] range=27-28 header=None
When Apollos decided to go on to Achaia province, his Ephesian friends gave their blessing and wrote a letter of recommendation for him, urging the disciples there to welcome him with open arms. The welcome paid off: Apollos turned out to be a great help to those who had become believers through God's immense generosity. He was particularly effective in public debate with the Jews as he brought out proof after convincing proof from the Scriptures that Jesus was in fact God's Messiah.

§Paul in Corinth

[EN 1] badge=1-4
So after his trip to Athens, Paul bounced over to Corinth. There, he met this awesome couple, Aquila and Priscilla. They’d just been kicked out of Italy because the emperor, Claudius, was being a total hater to all the Jews in Rome. Paul crashed with them, and since they were all tentmakers, they started a little business together. Every weekend, Paul would hit up the local synagogue and try to get everyone, Jews and Greeks, hyped about Jesus.

[EN 2] badge=5-6
When his buddies Silas and Timothy showed up from Macedonia, Paul went full-time into preaching mode. He was all in, trying to show the Jews that Jesus was the real deal, the Messiah they'd been waiting for. But they were not having it. They just argued and dissed everything he said. Paul got so fed up he was like, “Alright, I’m out. You guys do you. I’m gonna go talk to people who actually want to listen.”

[EN 3] badge=7-11
He bounced and went to hang out at the house of Titius Justus, a man who was super into God and lived right next door to the synagogue. But it wasn’t a total wash with the Jews, ‘cause the head of the synagogue, Crispus, and his whole family started believing in Jesus. And a ton of other people in Corinth who heard Paul’s message believed and got baptized. One night, Jesus came to Paul in a dream and was like, “Don’t stop. Don’t let anyone shut you up. I’ve got your back, and no one’s gonna mess with you. I’ve got a huge squad in this city.” That was all Paul needed to hear. He stuck around for another year and a half, teaching everyone about God.

[EN 4] badge=None
§Paul Before Gallio

[EN 5] badge=12-14
But then, when a new governor named Gallio was in charge, some of the Jews ganged up on Paul and dragged him to court. They were like, “This guy is trying to get people to worship God in a way that’s against the rules!”

[EN 6] badge=15-17
Just as Paul was about to say something, Gallio cut in and told the Jews, “Look, if he’d committed a crime, I’d listen. But this is just you guys arguing about your religion. Deal with it yourselves. I’m not wasting my time with this.” And he kicked them out of the courtroom. Then, the crowd grabbed the new synagogue leader, Sosthenes, and beat him up right there in front of everyone. Gallio just shrugged, like he couldn’t care less.

[EN 7] badge=18-19
Paul hung out in Corinth for a while longer, then it was time to go. He said bye to his friends and sailed to Syria with Priscilla and Aquila. Before they left, he shaved his head to show he was keeping a promise he’d made to God.

[EN 8] badge=19-21
They landed in Ephesus, and Priscilla and Aquila stayed there. Paul popped into the synagogue to talk to the Jews for a bit. They wanted him to stay longer, but he was like, “Nah, I gotta go. But I’ll come back if God’s cool with it.”

[EN 9] badge=None
§Back to Antioch

[EN 10] badge=22-23
From there, he sailed to Caesarea, said hi to the church, and then went on to Antioch. After chilling with the Christians in Antioch for a while, Paul hit the road again, visiting all the towns in Galatia and Phrygia and getting all the believers fired up.

[EN 11] badge=24-28
Meanwhile, this guy named Apollos showed up in Ephesus. He was from Alexandria in Egypt and was a super-talented speaker who knew the Scriptures inside and out. He was all about Jesus and taught everything he knew, but he only knew up to what John the Baptist had taught. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they pulled him aside and gave him the full scoop. When Apollos decided to go to Achaia, his new friends in Ephesus wrote him a letter of recommendation, telling the believers there to welcome him. And it was a good thing they did, because he was a huge help to them. He would publicly refute his opponents, using the Scriptures to prove that Jesus was the Messiah.
