[EN] Now for all the stuff living in the water. If it has fins and scales, you're good to go. ----- EN para 8 [29-38] -----
[MSG 29-38] "Among the creatures that crawl on the ground, the following are unclean for you: weasel, rat, all lizards, gecko, monitor lizard, wall lizard, skink, chameleon. Among the crawling creatures, these are unclean for you. If you touch them when they are dead, you are ritually unclean until evening. When one of them dies and falls on something, that becomes unclean no matter what it's used for, whether it's made of wood, cloth, hide, or sackcloth. Put it in the water—it's unclean until evening, and then it's clean. If one of these dead creatures falls into a clay pot, everything in the pot is unclean and you must break the pot. Any food that could be eaten but has water on it from such a pot is unclean, and any liquid that could be drunk from it is unclean. Anything that one of these carcasses falls on is unclean—an oven or cooking pot must be broken up; they're unclean and must be treated as unclean. A spring, though, or a cistern for collecting water remains clean, but if you touch one of these carcasses you're ritually unclean. If a carcass falls on any seeds that are to be planted, they remain clean. But if water has been put on the seed and a carcass falls on it, you must treat it as unclean.
[EN] As for the little critters that crawl on the ground, these are the ones you need to avoid: weasels, rats, all types of lizards, geckos, monitor lizards, wall lizards, skinks, and chameleons. These crawling things are unclean. If you touch one of them after it's dead, you're spiritually unclean until evening. If one of them dies and falls on something, that thing becomes unclean, no matter what it is—wood, cloth, leather, whatever. You have to put it in water, and it stays unclean until evening, then it's clean again. If a dead critter falls into a clay pot, everything in that pot is now unclean, and you have to smash the pot. Any food that could have been eaten becomes unclean if water from that pot touches it, and any drink in that pot is also unclean. Anything a carcass falls on becomes unclean. An oven or a pot has to be broken. They're unclean and you have to treat them that way. But a spring or a cistern where you collect water stays clean. Still, if you touch one of the carcasses in it, you become unclean. If a carcass falls on seeds you were going to plant, the seeds are still clean. But if the seeds were wet and a carcass falls on them, then they're unclean.
