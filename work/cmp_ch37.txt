===== CHAPTER 37: Joseph's Dreams Get Him Into Big Trouble (25 EN, 25 KO, 22 MSG) =====
[EN0 1] Jacob's family had settled in Canaan, the same place his dad, Isaac, had lived. This is where the story of Jacob’s family really kicks off.
[EN1 None] § Joseph the Dreamer
[EN2 2] It all started with Joseph, who was seventeen. He was out with his brothers, who were shepherds. These were his half-brothers, the sons of his dad’s other wives, Bilhah and Zilpah. Joseph wasn't a snitch, but he did tell his dad about some of the dumb stuff his brothers were doing.
[EN3 3-4] Now, their dad, Israel, loved Joseph more than any of his other kids because he was born when he was an old man. He even had a super fancy, colorful coat made just for him. When his brothers saw how much their dad favored Joseph, they got super jealous and started hating on him. They wouldn't even talk to him.
[EN4 5-7] Then Joseph had a dream, and when he told his brothers about it, they hated him even more. "Yo, check out this crazy dream I had," he said. "We were all in a field, tying up bundles of wheat. Suddenly, my bundle stood up straight, and all of your bundles gathered around and bowed down to mine."
[EN5 8] His brothers were like, "Seriously? You think you're going to be our king and boss us around?" That just made them hate him more, because of his dreams and the arrogant way he talked about them.
[EN6 9-10] But it didn't stop there. Joseph had another dream and, of course, he told his brothers all about it. "I had another dream! This time, the sun, the moon, and eleven stars were all bowing down to me."
[EN7 11] When he told his dad and brothers, his dad was like, "What is with all these dreams? You think me, your mom, and all your brothers are going to bow down to you?" His brothers were officially jealous, but his dad couldn't stop thinking about it.
[EN8 None] § The Betrayal
[EN9 12-14] One day, Joseph's brothers went to a place called Shechem to let their dad's sheep graze. So, Israel said to Joseph, "Your brothers are in Shechem with the flocks. I need you to go check on them."
[EN10 13] "I'm on it," Joseph said.
[EN11 14] "Go see how your brothers and the sheep are doing, and then come back and tell me," his dad said. And with that, he sent Joseph off from the Hebron Valley toward Shechem.
[EN12 15] Joseph was wandering around the fields when a man found him and asked, "What are you looking for?"
[EN13 15-16] "I'm looking for my brothers," Joseph said. "Do you know where they're grazing their flocks?"
[EN14 17] "They're not here anymore," the man said. "I heard them say they were going to Dothan." So Joseph went after his brothers and found them in Dothan.
[EN15 18-20] They saw him coming from a long way off, and by the time he got there, they had already come up with a plan to kill him. "Here comes the dreamer," they said to each other. "Let's kill him and dump his body in one of these old wells. We can just say a wild animal got him. Then we'll see what happens to his precious dreams."
[EN16 21-22] But Reuben, the oldest brother, heard them and tried to save Joseph. "We're not going to kill him," he said. "No bloodshed. Just throw him in this well out here in the middle of nowhere, but don't hurt him." Reuben was secretly planning to come back later, rescue Joseph, and take him back to their dad.
[EN17 23-24] When Joseph got to his brothers, they ripped off his fancy coat, grabbed him, and threw him into the well. It was completely dry; there was no water in it.
[EN18 25-27] Then they all sat down to eat. As they were eating, they saw a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead. Their camels were loaded up with spices and perfumes to sell in Egypt. That's when Judah said, "Guys, what do we get out of killing our brother and hiding the body? Let's just sell him to these Ishmaelites. At least we won't have killed him. He's our brother, our own flesh and blood, after all." And his brothers actually agreed.
[EN19 28] Just then, some Midianite traders passed by. So the brothers pulled Joseph out of the well and sold him for twenty pieces of silver to the Ishmaelites, who took him with them to Egypt.
[EN20 29-30] Later, Reuben came back to the well, and Joseph was gone! He tore his clothes, totally freaking out. He went to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone! What am I going to do?"
[EN21 31-32] They took Joseph's fancy coat, killed a goat, and dipped the coat in its blood. Then they took the coat back to their dad and said, "We found this. Take a look. Is this your son's coat?"
[EN22 33] He recognized it right away. "It's my son's coat! A wild animal must have eaten him. Joseph has been torn to pieces!"
[EN23 34-35] Jacob tore his clothes in grief, put on some rough clothes, and mourned for his son for a very long time. His other sons and daughters tried to comfort him, but he wouldn't listen. "I'll be mourning for my son until the day I die," he said. He just couldn't stop crying for him.
[EN24 36] Meanwhile, in Egypt, the Midianites sold Joseph to Potiphar, who was one of Pharaoh's officials and the captain of his guard.

[MSG0 37] Meanwhile Jacob had settled down where his father had lived, the land of Canaan.
[MSG1 2] This is the story of Jacob. The story continues with Joseph, seventeen years old at the time, helping out his brothers in herding the flocks. These were his half brothers actually, the sons of his father’s wives Bilhah and Zilpah. And Joseph brought his father bad reports on them.
[MSG2 3-4] Israel loved Joseph more than any of his other sons because he was the child of his old age. And he made him an elaborately embroidered coat. When his brothers realized that their father loved him more than them, they grew to hate him—they wouldn’t even speak to him.
[MSG3 5-7] Joseph had a dream. When he told it to his brothers, they hated him even more. He said, “Listen to this dream I had. We were all out in the field gathering bundles of wheat. All of a sudden my bundle stood straight up and your bundles circled around it and bowed down to mine.”
[MSG4 8] His brothers said, “So! You’re going to rule us? You’re going to boss us around?” And they hated him more than ever because of his dreams and the way he talked.
[MSG5 9] He had another dream and told this one also to his brothers: “I dreamed another dream—the sun and moon and eleven stars bowed down to me!”
[MSG6 10-11] When he told it to his father and brothers, his father reprimanded him: “What’s with all this dreaming? Am I and your mother and your brothers all supposed to bow down to you?” Now his brothers were really jealous; but his father brooded over the whole business.
[MSG7 12-13] His brothers had gone off to Shechem where they were pasturing their father’s flocks. Israel said to Joseph, “Your brothers are with flocks in Shechem. Come, I want to send you to them.” Joseph said, “I’m ready.”
[MSG8 14] He said, “Go and see how your brothers and the flocks are doing and bring me back a report.” He sent him off from the valley of Hebron to Shechem.
[MSG9 15] A man met him as he was wandering through the fields and asked him, “What are you looking for?”
[MSG10 16] “I’m trying to find my brothers. Do you have any idea where they are grazing their flocks?”
[MSG11 17] The man said, “They’ve left here, but I overheard them say, ‘Let’s go to Dothan.’” So Joseph took off, tracked his brothers down, and found them in Dothan.
[MSG12 18-20] They spotted him off in the distance. By the time he got to them they had cooked up a plot to kill him. The brothers were saying, “Here comes that dreamer. Let’s kill him and throw him into one of these old cisterns; we can say that a vicious animal ate him up. We’ll see what his dreams amount to.”
[MSG13 21-22] Reuben heard the brothers talking and intervened to save him, “We’re not going to kill him. No murder. Go ahead and throw him in this cistern out here in the wild, but don’t hurt him.” Reuben planned to go back later and get him out and take him back to his father.
[MSG14 23-24] When Joseph reached his brothers, they ripped off the fancy coat he was wearing, grabbed him, and threw him into a cistern. The cistern was dry; there wasn’t any water in it.
[MSG15 25-27] Then they sat down to eat their supper. Looking up, they saw a caravan of Ishmaelites on their way from Gilead, their camels loaded with spices, ointments, and perfumes to sell in Egypt. Judah said, “Brothers, what are we going to get out of killing our brother and concealing the evidence? Let’s sell him to the Ishmaelites, but let’s not kill him—he is, after all, our brother, our own flesh and blood.” His brothers agreed.
[MSG16 28] By that time the Midianite traders were passing by. His brothers pulled Joseph out of the cistern and sold him for twenty pieces of silver to the Ishmaelites who took Joseph with them down to Egypt.
[MSG17 29-30] Later Reuben came back and went to the cistern—no Joseph! He ripped his clothes in despair. Beside himself, he went to his brothers. “The boy’s gone! What am I going to do!”
[MSG18 31-32] They took Joseph’s coat, butchered a goat, and dipped the coat in the blood. They took the fancy coat back to their father and said, “We found this. Look it over—do you think this is your son’s coat?”
[MSG19 33] He recognized it at once. “My son’s coat—a wild animal has eaten him. Joseph torn limb from limb!”
[MSG20 34-35] Jacob tore his clothes in grief, dressed in rough burlap, and mourned his son a long, long time. His sons and daughters tried to comfort him but he refused their comfort. “I’ll go to the grave mourning my son.” Oh, how his father wept for him.
[MSG21 36] In Egypt the Midianites sold Joseph to Potiphar, one of Pharaoh’s officials, manager of his household affairs.
