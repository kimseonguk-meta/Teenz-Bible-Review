===== CHAPTER 50: Joseph's Final Act: Forgiveness and a Promise (22 EN, 22 KO, 16 MSG) =====
[EN0 1] Joseph just broke down. He threw himself on his father, cried his eyes out, and kissed him goodbye.
[EN1 None] § Saying Goodbye to Dad
[EN2 2-3] Then, Joseph told the doctors who worked for him to get his dad’s body ready for burial, Egyptian-style. So they embalmed Jacob, which took the full 40 days. The whole country of Egypt mourned for him for 70 days straight.
[EN3 4-6] When the official mourning time was over, Joseph went to Pharaoh's officials and said, “Hey, if you guys think I’m cool, could you do me a solid and pass this message to Pharaoh? My dad made me promise him something super serious. He said, ‘Look, I’m about to die. You have to bury me in the tomb I got ready for myself back in Canaan.’ So, can you please let me go and bury my dad? I’ll come right back, for real.”
[EN4 6] Pharaoh was like, “Of course. Go bury your father, just like you promised him.”
[EN5 None] § The Long Road to Canaan
[EN6 7-10] So Joseph headed out to bury his father. And it was a huge deal. All of Pharaoh’s top advisors, all the big shots in Egypt, they all went with him. Joseph’s whole family went too—his brothers and everyone. They just left their kids and their animals back in Goshen. They even had chariots and horsemen with them. It was a massive funeral procession, like something out of a movie.
[EN7 10-11] When they got to the Atad Threshing Floor, which is just across the Jordan River, they stopped and had a huge mourning ceremony. It was super loud and went on for a while. For seven days, Joseph led these funeral rituals for his dad.
[EN8 11] The local Canaanites saw all this intense grieving and were like, “Whoa, the Egyptians are mourning like crazy.” That’s actually how that place got its name: Abel Mizraim, which means “Egyptian Mourning.”
[EN9 12-14] Jacob’s sons did exactly what their dad told them to do. They carried him into Canaan and buried him in the cave in the field of Machpelah, near Mamre. This was the same field Abraham had bought from Ephron the Hittite as a burial spot.
[EN10 None] § Brothers Get Scared
[EN11 15] After the funeral, Joseph and his brothers and everyone who came with them went back to Egypt. But once they were back, his brothers started freaking out. They were talking to each other like, “Uh oh. What if Joseph is still holding a grudge? What if he decides to get us back for all the messed-up stuff we did to him?”
[EN12 16-17] So they sent a message to Joseph that said, “Yo, before he died, our dad told us to tell you this: ‘Please, forgive your brothers for the terrible thing they did to you. It was really bad.’ So, will you? Can you forgive us? We’re all servants of the same God your father worshipped.”
[EN13 17] When Joseph read their message, he started crying.
[EN14 18] Then his brothers came to him in person, bowed down flat on the ground, and said, “We’ll be your slaves. For real.”
[EN15 None] § Joseph's Big Speech
[EN16 19-21] But Joseph told them, “Chill out, guys. Don’t be scared. Do I look like God to you? Look, you totally planned to do something evil to me, but God flipped it and used your plan for something good. And the result is right in front of you—all these people are alive because of it. So seriously, don’t worry. You have nothing to be afraid of. I’ll take care of you and your kids.” He talked to them from the heart and made them feel a lot better.
[EN17 None] § The End of an Era
[EN18 22-23] Joseph stayed in Egypt with his whole family and lived to be 110 years old. He got to see his great-grandkids from his son Ephraim. And the kids of his grandson Makir (from his other son Manasseh) were also considered part of his family.
[EN19 24] When he knew his time was up, Joseph said to his brothers, “I’m about to die. But God is definitely going to come through for you. He’ll lead you out of this country and take you back to the land he promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.”
[EN20 25] Then Joseph made them all swear an oath: “When God shows up to get you, you have to promise me you’ll take my bones with you when you leave.”
[EN21 26] Joseph died when he was 110. They embalmed him and put him in a coffin in Egypt.
----- MSG -----
[MSG0 50] Joseph threw himself on his father, wept over him, and kissed him.
[MSG1 2-3] Joseph then instructed the physicians in his employ to embalm his father. The physicians embalmed Israel. The embalming took forty days, the period required for embalming. There was public mourning by the Egyptians for seventy days.
[MSG2 4-5] When the period of mourning was completed, Joseph petitioned Pharaoh’s court: “If you have reason to think kindly of me, present Pharaoh with my request: My father made me swear, saying, ‘I am ready to die. Bury me in the grave plot that I prepared for myself in the land of Canaan.’ Please give me leave to go up and bury my father. Then I’ll come back.”
[MSG3 6] Pharaoh said, “Certainly. Go and bury your father as he made you promise under oath.”
[MSG4 7-9] So Joseph left to bury his father. And all the high-ranking officials from Pharaoh’s court went with him, all the dignitaries of Egypt, joining Joseph’s family—his brothers and his father’s family. Their children and flocks and herds were left in Goshen. Chariots and horsemen accompanied them. It was a huge funeral procession.
[MSG5 10] Arriving at the Atad Threshing Floor just across the Jordan River, they stopped for a period of mourning, letting their grief out in loud and lengthy lament. For seven days, Joseph engaged in these funeral rites for his father.
[MSG6 11] When the Canaanites who lived in that area saw the grief being poured out at the Atad Threshing Floor, they said, “Look how deeply the Egyptians are mourning.” That is how the site at the Jordan got the name Abel Mizraim (Egyptian Lament).
[MSG7 12-13] Jacob’s sons continued to carry out his instructions to the letter. They took him on into Canaan and buried him in the cave in the field of Machpelah facing Mamre, the field that Abraham had bought as a burial plot from Ephron the Hittite.
[MSG8 14-15] After burying his father, Joseph went back to Egypt. All his brothers who had come with him to bury his father returned with him. After the funeral, Joseph’s brothers talked among themselves: “What if Joseph is carrying a grudge and decides to pay us back for all the wrong we did him?”
[MSG9 16-17] So they sent Joseph a message, “Before his death, your father gave this command: Tell Joseph, ‘Forgive your brothers’ sin—all that wrongdoing. They did treat you very badly.’ Will you do it? Will you forgive the sins of the servants of your father’s God?” When Joseph received their message, he wept.
[MSG10 18] Then the brothers went in person to him, threw themselves on the ground before him and said, “We’ll be your slaves.”
[MSG11 19-21] Joseph replied, “Don’t be afraid. Do I act for God? Don’t you see, you planned evil against me but God used those same plans for my good, as you see all around you right now—life for many people. Easy now, you have nothing to fear; I’ll take care of you and your children.” He reassured them, speaking with them heart-to-heart.
[MSG12 22-23] Joseph continued to live in Egypt with his father’s family. Joseph lived 110 years. He lived to see Ephraim’s sons into the third generation. The sons of Makir, Manasseh’s son, were also recognized as Joseph’s.
[MSG13 24] At the end, Joseph said to his brothers, “I am ready to die. God will most certainly pay you a visit and take you out of this land and back to the land he so solemnly promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.”
[MSG14 25] Then Joseph made the sons of Israel promise under oath, “When God makes his visitation, make sure you take my bones with you as you leave here.”
[MSG15 26] Joseph died at the age of 110 years. They embalmed him and placed him in a coffin in Egypt.
